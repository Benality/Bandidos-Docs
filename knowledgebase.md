## bans

## Bans & Disciplinary Process

### 📝 How We Handle Reports

- All **player reports** are reviewed by the Iron Range staff team as quickly as possible.
- Players involved in a report may be contacted for clarification. The goal is to **understand the context**, not to automatically punish.
- When appropriate, staff will offer **guidance and education** before issuing disciplinary action.

### 🤝 Conflict Resolution First

- Whenever possible, our goal is to **encourage resolution between players**, not escalation.
- Some reports may result in a **staff-mediated conversation** if both parties are open to it.
- Players who consistently show a willingness to learn and adapt will always be treated with more leniency.

### ⚖️ Disciplinary Actions

- Staff may issue **warnings, suspensions, or permanent bans** depending on the nature of the rulebreak.
- Bans may be temporary (e.g., 3-day, 7-day, 30-day) or permanent depending on **severity, history, and attitude**.
- If a player openly shares misleading or incomplete information about their ban, staff may respond with public clarification and evidence to protect community trust.

### 📬 Ban Appeals

- Players banned for **7 days or longer** may submit one appeal, if there was information not reviewed during the intial decision.
- Appeals must show **growth, accountability, and respect** for the community and its expectations.
- Appeal instructions will be provided at the time of your ban.
- Not all bans are permitted to appeal

### 🔒 Confidentiality

- The outcome of all reports and staff decisions is considered **confidential** between the involved parties and staff.
- However, if a banned player chooses to go public, **staff may share additional context or evidence** when necessary to correct misinformation.

### ❗ Zero Tolerance Violations

The following actions will result in immediate and permanent removal from the community:

- Cheating, hacking, or exploit abuse
- Harassment or abuse toward players or staff
- Sexual misconduct or erotic roleplay
- Suicide or self-harm roleplay
- Repeated targeted toxicity, even after warnings
- "Troll content" or any action aimed with the sole purpose of disrupting roleplay

## character

## 👤 Character Policy

Iron Range is a story-driven server built on meaningful, grounded character creation. All characters should feel authentic to the world of **1880s Horizen** and contribute to immersive, long-term storytelling.

---

### 🔞 Character Age Requirement

- All characters must be **18 years of age or older**, both physically and mentally.
- Characters under 18 — even implied in backstory — **may not** be played or referenced in active roleplay.
- **Pregnancy roleplay is not allowed.** Pregnancy may exist in backstory only, but not be actively roleplayed on-screen.

---

### 🧬 Unique & Original Characters

- Characters must be **original creations**.
- You may draw inspiration from history or fiction, but **do not copy** real people or fictional characters.
- **No celebrity names, Red Dead Redemption characters, or famous outlaws** (e.g., Billy the Kid, John Marston).
- This also applies to **gangs and businesses** — names and themes must be original.

---

### 🤝 Shared Backstories

- You may create family ties or backstory connections **only with prior OOC consent** from the other player(s).
- Shared backstories should enhance your character's story — not exist solely to **revive old conflicts** from other servers.
- Each character should be **narratively independent**, with their own motivations and goals.

---

### 🧠 Memory & Continuity

- Characters from other servers may be **loosely reimagined**, but their **memories must be wiped**.
  - No references to old names, places, events, or feuds.
  - General terms like “another state” are fine — but keep it vague and disconnected.
- Horizen is a **fresh world**, unrelated to Red Dead lore. The East is unexplored, and the past is unwritten.

---

### 🕰️ Character Aging

- Characters should age **slowly and realistically**, in line with server time.
- If you retire a character for 6+ months IRL, you may age them slightly upon return.
- Unrealistic time jumps (e.g., “my child is now an adult overnight”) are not allowed unless approved by staff.

---

### 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Alternate Characters (Alts)

- **Each alt character must be entirely unique** — with different identities, motivations, and social circles.
- **Sharing resources between your characters is not allowed.**
  - This includes money, items, property, horses, stashes, and weapons.
  - **This is considered powergaming.**

> If you believe there's an **exceptional RP reason** to share resources between alts (e.g. inherited property, family-run shop), you must get **staff approval** in advance.

⚠️ Things get complicated fast with **large groups, families, or gangs** that include multiple alts.  
If it feels questionable — ask first.

---

### 🚫 Forbidden Character Types & Themes

The following are not allowed:

- Child or teen characters (including overly childish behavior)
- Pregnancy RP or active pregnancies
- Abuse or trauma centered on children or sexual violence
- Recreated characters that continue old feuds
- Supernatural powers or entities

---

Characters are the foundation of Iron Range.  
Build someone who could thrive (or struggle) in the harsh world of Horizen — and let the story unfold naturally.

## characterdeletion

## Character Deletion Policy

To maintain server performance and support a dynamic world, **Iron Range automatically deletes inactive characters** after a certain period.

---

### ⏳ Inactivity Timeout

- **Any character that has not logged in for 60 days will be automatically deleted.**
- This applies to all characters, regardless of status or progression.
- There are no exceptions unless explicitly granted by staff ahead of time.

---

### 💤 Inactive Status (Extended Timeout)

If you know you'll be away for an extended period (travel, real life, etc.), you may request to have your character marked as **Inactive**:

- This extends the deletion window to **120 days**.
- After 120 days, your character **will still be deleted** per policy.
- Contact support via a **Discord ticket** to request inactive status before reaching the 60-day mark.

> Inactive status gives you extra time — but it's not permanent.

---

### 🔥 What Gets Deleted

When a character is deleted, **all associated data is permanently removed**, including:

- Bank accounts
- Telegram address book
- Inventory and crafted items
- Clothing and outfits
- Horses, saddles, and wagons
- Owned businesses or player-run shops
- Weapon presets and stashes
- Any other property or resource linked to that character

> This includes everything your character owns or has created. It is **not recoverable**.

---

### 🔄 Recovery Policy

If your character is deleted due to inactivity and you wish to return:

- **Submit a support ticket in the Discord.**
- Staff can **restore the character’s name, appearance, and backstory.**
- However, **no items, weapons, money, or property will be restored.**

> You’ll have your character back — but you’ll need to **start fresh** in terms of resources.

---

### ⚠️ Manual Deletion Requests

Due to the complexity of properly removing all associated character data,  
**we do not field manual character deletion requests**.

- Our automated system ensures all related data is fully and cleanly removed.
- If you wish to delete your character, simply stop logging into them.
- After 60 days of inactivity (or 120 with inactive status), the system will handle deletion automatically.

---

### 🧭 Final Note

Our world is built on **active characters and meaningful stories**.  
If you want to preserve your legacy — log in, stay active, or let us know when you need a break.

## cooldowns

## Story Pacing & Conflict Cooldowns

Iron Range is a story-first server where arcs unfold over weeks—not minutes. We value breathing room, emotional weight, and time for players to react. This section outlines expectations for pacing your conflict and allowing story beats to land.

---

### 🧠 Don’t Chain Major Events

- If your character just:
  - Robbed a bank
  - Got in a major shootout
  - Broke out of jail
  - Kidnapped someone

You should **not immediately dive into another serious crime**.

Give time for:

- Investigations to begin
- Rumors to spread
- Emotions to settle

> A rushed second event often waters down the impact of the first.

---

### 📆 General Conflict Cooldowns

These are **minimums** based on the same character (or alternate characters under your control) involved in similar events.

| Event Type               | Cooldown (per character) |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Bank Robbery             | 4 days                   |
| Train Robbery            | 4 days                   |
| Jailbreak                | 4 days (non-gang)        |
| Gang Conflict Escalation | 3 days minimum           |
| Major Kidnapping         | 2 days                   |

> **Note:** These cooldowns may be extended if staff notice abuse or if the story feels rushed.

---

### 🔁 Using Alternate Characters

- You **may not swap to another character** to bypass a cooldown on conflict or robbery.
- Staff will track cooldown abuse across all characters tied to the same player if necessary.

---

### 🔥 Let Things Breathe

- Give law time to investigate.
- Let your gang stew on betrayal or loss.
- Let rumors grow in town.
- Consider how your last move could change the world—and then reflect that change before moving again.

---

### 🎭 Impact Over Action

- A single, slow-burning storyline often has **more impact** than rapid, stacked scenes.
- Let others get involved, ask questions, and build connections through your story.
- Roleplay is not about “what’s next”—it’s about “what does it mean?”

---

When everything is fast, nothing feels important.  
Slow down. Let tension simmer. That’s where the best stories live.

## criminalactivity

## Criminal Activity Rules

Criminal activity in Iron Range is meant to be **high-stakes**, **story-driven**, and **player-led**. These events should push forward rivalries, build tension with law, and reward well-planned risk—not casual grinding or win-focused play.

---

### 🗝️ Jailbreaks & Escaping from Custody

- Once a wanted character is in **custody or inside a jail cell**, any attempt to break them out counts as a **jailbreak**.
- Jailbreaks must be:
  - **Story-driven** — not repeated for convenience or farming action
  - **Reasonable** — avoid jailbreaking someone you barely know
  - **Well-paced** — don’t immediately jailbreak someone who just got caught

#### 🔁 Cooldowns

- **Gang Members** may jailbreak their own at any time—no cooldowns apply—but staff may step in if it’s abused. Make sure to follow above guidelines.
- **Non-gang characters** must wait **4 days** before initiating another jailbreak.
- You cannot jailbreak with one character, then do it again on another during that cooldown period.

> **Note:** Law enforcement is encouraged to respond seriously to jailbreaks and may escalate their investigations accordingly.

---

### 🏦 Bank Robberies

- A maximum of **6 criminal players** may participate in a single bank robbery.
- Once the robbery starts (e.g., breach initiated), the robbery is considered **in progress**.

#### 📆 Robbery Cooldowns

- Each player may only rob **one bank every 4 days**.
- If you rob a bank on one character, you **cannot** rob another on a different character during that cooldown.

#### 👥 Third-Party Interference

- No third parties may interfere with the robbery—either to assist or oppose.
- Always keep in mind the conflict limits policy.

#### 🏃 Aftermath Expectations

- Getaways should be immersive—not cinematic speedruns.
- If you escape the law, you’re expected to **lay low**. Jumping into another crime immediately may be flagged for **poor pacing**.

---

### 🚂 Train Robberies

- A maximum of **6 criminal players** may rob a train.
- Robbers may **board ahead of time in disguise**, but must clearly announce their intent **once the robbery begins**.

#### 🎭 Role of Law

- Responding law may ask how many outlaws and hostages are present. Robbers AND law, are under no obligation to disclose how many people are in each group. However, it required the groups are aware of and follow the group-size limits.
  PCs.

---

Criminal scenes should be **tense, risky, and full of story**.  
Every job should have **consequences**, **reactions**, and a **reason** to be remembered.

## dueling

## Dueling Policy

Duels are a part of Western tradition and can be a powerful way to settle disputes—when done with purpose. Iron Range supports duels as **story-driven events**, not casual PvP.

### 🪶 What Counts as a Duel?

A duel is defined as any **one-on-one agreed combat**, usually:

- To settle a personal or ideological conflict
- To resolve tension without escalating into a full group war
- As a challenge to authority, honor, or legacy

Duels may be with:

- Pistols
- Melee weapons
- Bare fists (for informal challenges)

---

### ✅ Duel Rules

- **Both parties must agree, following initiation** before the duel begins.
- A **witness is recommended** for each side, but not required.
- Duels must have **build-up**.
- Once agreed upon, **the duel is final**. The outcome should be respected.

#### 🧠 NVL Still Applies

- The loser of a duel must **value their life** and follow through on consequences—this may include:
  - Surrendering land, items, or titles
  - Leaving town or disbanding a gang
  - Facing death or exile, if previously agreed

#### ⚠️ No Duel Loopholes

- You may not force a duel, or not give the other party any other option but to duel or face negative consquences.
- Dueling to bait someone into a PvP scene without consent will be treated as **meta or powergaming**.

---

### 💀 Duels & Perma

- Perma is never required, even in a duel to the death.
- If both parties **agree in advance** that perma is on the table, it must be clear in OOC beforehand.
- Otherwise, the defeated player decides the outcome:
  - Near-death or long-term injury
  - Capture, exile, or other non-lethal consequence

---

In Iron Range, a duel is a statement. It should reflect your character’s pride, pain, or desperation—  
not a shortcut to avoid consequences or push PvP.

## groupsizes

## Group Size Limits

To maintain balance, story diversity, and server performance, Iron Range enforces reasonable group size expectations—especially during **conflict** or **server-wide events**.

### ⚔️ PvP & Conflict Limits

- A **maximum of 6 armed participants per group** may engage in a PvP scene at any given time.
- This includes:
  - Shootouts
  - Ambushes
  - Duels involving more than one group
- Any additional group members must **stay out of combat** and either watch, stay in cover, or leave the area.

### 🕊️ Travel & Social RP

- Groups **can travel or socialize in larger numbers** as long as they are not entering or escalating conflict.
- However, be mindful of server performance and fairness—especially if you're entering a known hotspot or rival territory.

---

### 🔫 Criminal Groups

- Criminal gangs may travel or operate in groups larger than 6, but:
  - Only 6 members can engage in **any single violent encounter**.
  - Large gang presence at events must not **dominate or overshadow others**.
- Criminal "hubs" (e.g., hideouts, encampments) are allowed to host more than 6 people—just not during PvP.

---

### 👮 Law, Civilians, and Factions

- Law and civilian groups are held to the same PvP cap: **6 armed participants max** in a single conflict.
- Larger faction events (e.g. patrols, town meetings) may involve more, but **only 6 may enter or respond to combat**.
- The rest must act in support roles or move out of the scene.

---

### 🧠 Use Common Sense

- Do not attempt to “stack” multiple groups to bypass limits.
- If law is engaging a 6-person outlaw group, **only one group of 6 law characters** should be active in that fight.
- Staff may review situations that feel lopsided or exploitative—even if numbers are technically within the rules.

---

This rule exists to create **cinematic, story-first conflict**, not tactical advantages through sheer numbers.  
Respect the cap, and give everyone a scene worth telling.

## hostages

## Hostage Rules

Hostage situations in Iron Range are meant to **elevate tension**, **drive roleplay**, and **create narrative stakes**. They are not to be used as props, bargaining chips without purpose, or means to bypass PvP mechanics.

### 🎭 Hostages Are Players Too

- A hostage must be **meaningfully included in the scene**, not just tied up and ignored.
- Interact with your hostage—threaten, bribe, or involve them in the stakes.
- Give them a moment to **react and participate**, even in fear or confusion.

### 🚨 Law Enforcement Hostages

- **On-duty law characters may not be held hostage** during major criminal events like:
  - Bank robberies
  - Train robberies
- If taken hostage during unrelated RP, they **must be released** before the event begins.
- This ensures law can **respond freely** to active scenes and prevents OOC balance issues.

### 🧠 Hostage Use Expectations

- **No “willing hostages”** or disguised gang members pretending to be hostages.
- If a character trades themselves in willingly, it must be supported by **roleplay history** or strong reasoning—not pre-planned coordination.
- Using a hostage to **stall or bait PvP** without roleplay will be treated as **meta or powergaming**.

### 🎤 Communication & Immersion

- Players acting as hostages should:

  - Stay in character
  - React appropriately to fear, pain, or pressure
  - Avoid meta-gaming or OOC complaints mid-scene

- Captors must:
  - Avoid mocking, dismissing, or silencing hostages without roleplay
  - Ensure the hostage can **express distress or respond** unless gagged

---

Hostage scenes are unforgettable when done right.  
Give them weight, give them purpose, and let everyone involved walk away with a story.

## incapacitation

## Incapacitation, Unconsciousness, & PermaDeath

Death and injury are natural outcomes in a world as dangerous as Iron Range. However, we treat these states with narrative respect, not as mechanical endpoints. You always have agency in how your character responds to being downed.

### 🩸 Incapacitation

- When your character's health reaches zero, you are **incapacitated**.
- You may choose to have your character remain **semi-conscious**, whispering or weakly responding to nearby players.
- You should only respond based on **what your character would reasonably perceive**—for example, blurred vision, muffled voices, etc.

### 💤 Unconsciousness

- If you choose to go **unconscious**, you **must not remember** anything that occurs while you're unconscious.
- You may slip in and out of consciousness, but this should be **clearly roleplayed** using `/me` or emotes.
- Whispering or speaking while unconscious is considered **fail RP** unless you’ve actively emoted waking up or regaining awareness.

### ⚰️ PermaDeath (Permas)

- You are never required to permanently kill your character—but if you choose to, it must be **final**.
- Use `/me is dead` or another clear emote if you are declaring your character’s death. Once this is done, there is **no coming back**.
- If you’re unsure, consider placing your character in a **coma** or critical state, and make the final call later.
- See the [Permadeath Command Usage](permadeath.md)

### 🔪 Executions & Forced Death

- **Players cannot force you to perma**. Even if another character emotes a killing blow, the choice to permanently end your character lies with you.
- Executions should be **used sparingly** and **only in climactic moments**. Abuse of execution mechanics will be considered NVL or powergaming.
- Execution animations are not for clearing downed players in combat. They are reserved for story-driven, deliberate conclusions.

### 🩻 Forced Injury / Mutilation RP

- Any injury that would result in **permanent physical consequences** (e.g. eye gouging, limb removal) **requires OOC consent** from the player.
- Use a "pigeon" or create a ticket to get consent OOC with the player. Staff is happy to be involved if needed.
- If consent is not given, the roleplay must stop or pivot in another direction.
- Do not continue to shoot the limbs (including head) of a downed player to mutilate their bodies.

### ✅ Staff Tracking

- If your character dies (perma) or suffers a serious event like an execution or coma, notify staff via the **Discord Support Bot** so we can maintain accurate story records.
- This is especially important for **Prison** or **medical roles**, where tracking outcomes helps maintain continuity.

---

Treat injury and death with narrative weight. Whether you go out in a blaze of glory or live to tell the tale, make it count for the story—and for those around you.

## itemlabels

## Item Label Rules in Iron Range

**Item Labels** are a special item used to assign a custom name and color to most items in the game. They are designed to enhance character-driven roleplay by making crafted or collected items feel personal and unique.

---

> Transient players are not permitted to use the Item Label

### ✅ Permitted Use

- Labels **can** be applied to most standard items.
- **They cannot be used** on:
  - Weapons.
  - Items that already have metadata (e.g., used tools like pickaxes or shovels).
  - However, tools **without durability set** (i.e., new) may be labeled.
- The label is automatically removed once durability or other metadata is applied.

---

### 🎭 Roleplay Enhancement

- Labels **do not affect item stats**, but they add _flavor_ and _uniqueness_ for roleplay.
- Example: While anyone can craft chicken and waffles, _“Dave’s Famous Chicken & Waffles”_ becomes a roleplay icon if the character is memorable and social.
- This system promotes player creativity, character branding, and immersive in-character businesses.

---

### 🧼 Content Guidelines

- All **standard server rules apply**, including restrictions on:
  - Racist or discriminatory language.

In the world of Iron Range, Indigenous cultures are alive, distinct, and integrated into the fictional world of Horizen. Iron Range is **not historical fiction**, and Horizen does not directly mirror real tribes or real events. However, inspiration from real Native cultures is welcome—as long as it’s done respectfully and without appropriation or stereotypes. All tribes in Horizen are fictional, but players are encouraged to create customs, beliefs, and roles that feel culturally consistent and immersive.

### 🚫 No Tolerance for Racism

We do **not allow racism**, hate speech, or derogatory portrayals—**in-character or out-of-character**. “Historical accuracy” is not a justification for bigotry. If you’re portraying conflict, it must be **consensual, story-driven, and respectful**.

Examples:

- ✅ You can explore cultural misunderstandings, tensions, or alliances with settlers if done maturely.
- ❌ You cannot use slurs, depict massacres, or treat Native characters as set dressing.

Offenses may result in immediate removal from the community.

### 🧬 Character Depth & Cultural Responsibility

Your Indigenous character should be a **full person**: flawed, evolving, and grounded in their beliefs or community. Avoid clichés like “stoic warrior” or “magical healer.”

Native characters can be:

- Hunters, traders, spiritual leaders, diplomats, artists, or outlaws.
- Members of tribes with original names and customs.
- Survivors who carry cultural teachings and adapt to the changing world.

### 🪶 Clothing, Names, and Culture

- Avoid costume-style outfits or randomly generated names like “Soaring Hawk.” Names and attire should reflect your character’s **tribal culture**, backstory, or life experience.
- War paint, headdresses, or ceremonial wear must have a **purpose**—spiritual, personal, or role-based. Don’t wear them casually in town.

If you are forming or joining a tribe:

### ⚖️ Conflict RP

- Conflict should be approached like any other character arc: with nuance, purpose, and collaboration.
- Betrayals, rivalries, or land disputes must be **story-based**, not used to justify excessive aggression or isolation.

Iron Range welcomes Native roleplay. We believe it can offer **beautiful, grounded, and powerful stories**. But with that opportunity comes responsibility. Don’t treat Native cultures as costume or aesthetic—**live them through immersive, thoughtful roleplay**.

If you're unsure about your portrayal, reach out to staff. We’re here to **support respectful creativity**, not to restrict it.

## nvl

## Value of Life (NVL)

In Iron Range, your character’s life should matter. Reckless, immersion-breaking behavior not only weakens the story—it disrupts others’ experience. The Value of Life (NVL) policy ensures characters act with self-preservation and realism in life-threatening situations.

### 🧠 Think Like a Real Person

- Your character should avoid actions that would **needlessly risk their life**.
- This includes:
  - Talking tough or mocking someone while tied up, outnumbered, or outgunned.
  - Running into a gunfight alone without reason.
  - Ignoring clear threats from predators, outlaws, or lawmen.
  - Treating injuries as minor inconveniences—limp, bleed, or pass out when appropriate.

### 🔫 Gunpoint Situations

- If someone has a weapon **already drawn and pointed at you**, you are expected to:
  - Comply realistically (e.g. raise hands, surrender, talk down).
  - Avoid instantly drawing a weapon, lassoing, or escalating without narrative reason.
- Drawing while at gunpoint without meaningful story or intent is likely NVL.

### 🧩 Justified Last Stands

- **Refusing to surrender**, even when outnumbered or cornered, **can be valid**—but only when it makes sense for the story and character.
- Staff will look at:
  - **Intent** – Was it for roleplay, not just to “win”?
  - **Build-up** – Was there proper tension or lead-in?
  - **Impact** – Did it add to the story or ruin the moment?

Characters may stand their ground out of **conviction, desperation, or legacy**—but not simply to avoid consequences or restart a fight.

### 🪂 Reckless Physical Behavior

- Do not:
  - Jump off cliffs, bridges, or buildings without clear motivation.
  - Run into certain death without an emotional or narrative hook.
  - Treat dangerous terrain or wild animals like minor threats.

### 🤐 Tied or Restrained

- If you’re hogtied, cuffed, or unconscious, your ability to act should reflect that.
- Breaking free while surrounded, or without roleplay, will be considered **powergaming or NVL**.
- See the **Restraints & Escape** section for more detailed guidance.

---

Not every character chooses to survive—but every character should treat life as if it has meaning.  
If your choices lead to death, make sure they tell a story worth remembering.

## playerrobberies

## Player Robbery Rules

Robberies in Iron Range are meant to create tension, drama, and long-term stories — **not loot grabs**. Every robbery should make sense for your character, fit the scene, and respect the server's story-first values.

---

### 🔫 You Can Rob, But You Cannot Kill

- Robbery should **not end in murder** unless the victim escalates (e.g., runs, fights back, or poses an immediate threat).
- Killing someone purely to ensure a clean robbery is **not allowed**.
- Let your robbery scene be **memorable, not terminal**.

---

### 🧍 Transient Player Protection

- **Transient (non-allowlisted) players**:
  - ❌ Cannot rob others
  - ❌ Cannot be robbed
  - ✅ Can be hostages or bystanders in robbery scenes

---

### 🎒 Backpack Rules

Backpacks offer utility and immersion, but come with responsibility. Misusing them in robberies or PvP scenes may result in administrative action.

- **Wearing a backpack** gives you extra storage that can be accessed at any time.
- **Placing it on the ground** allows others to **pick it up or search it**.
- You **cannot store the backpack** if it contains items — it must be empty first.

#### 🚨 During Robberies:

- **Do not move items** to/from your backpack _while being actively robbed_ in order to avoid losing valuables.
  - You **may** move items if you **escape or evade** and have time to do so.
  - This should be done to **support roleplay**, not to "win" the situation.
- Backpacks are **automatically dropped when a player dies**.
  - Killing a player **just to loot the backpack** is not allowed.
- Robbers may **order a victim to drop their backpack**.
  - This **does not violate** the “one source” robbery rule — backpacks are considered an **extension of the person**.
  - Victims must **comply and roleplay the situation appropriately**, per NVL and story-first expectations.

---

### 🔫 Weapon Theft Rules

You may only steal a weapon if:

- It is **visibly equipped**, or
- It was **used recently** in the current or connected scene

> You **cannot** steal a weapon just because you know the player has one or saw it OOC days ago.

---

When searching a player, take care to take only the weapon(s) that you visibly see or know the character has from a connected scene.

#### 🎯 What is a “Connected Scene”?

A **connected scene** means there is a clear, recent roleplay link between your character and the weapon in question.

**✅ Example of a connected scene:**

> You spoke with someone earlier in town. They mentioned heading out to hunt with their Rolling Block.  
> Later, you find them in the wilderness. That is a valid IC connection — you **know** they have the Rolling Block.

**❌ Example of a non-connected scene:**

> You saw the same person with a Rolling Block the day before, but there’s been no interaction or continuity.  
> You cannot assume they still have the same weapon unless you build fresh RP around it.

Use conversation to build this connection. Example:

> _“Hey, how’s that Rolling Block treating you? Bag anything good the other day?”_  
> — This gives the other player an opportunity to confirm, deny, or redirect based on their RP.

---

If you **do search** and find additional weapons, and wish to **disarm the player**, that is allowed. However:

- Any extra weapons **should be returned** to the player **after the scene**, unless they are eligible to be stolen (per the above rules).
- You may also use:  
  `/me disarms all weapons` — This is an RP indicator that your character is **taking all weapons for the duration of the scene**.
  - The victim **must respect this RP indicator**.

#### ⚠️ One Weapon Per Player

- Each **victim** can only lose **one weapon**, regardless of how many robbers are present.
- Coordinate with your group — do not “multi-strip” a victim.

---

### 🐎 Horse, Wagon, or Person — Pick One

When robbing a player, your group may:

- **Search their person**
- **Search their horse**
- **Search their wagon**

But:  
You may **only steal from one of those**.  
If you take from their person, you cannot also loot their horse or wagon.

> ✅ This rule applies **per victim**, not per robber.

---

### 💰 Money, Items, and Intent

- You may take **on-person cash** if it fits the scene.
- **Do not wipe inventories**. Take only what your character would reasonably want.
- **Stealing is not the goal. Story is.**

Ask yourself:

- “Is this building tension?”
- “Will this create lasting consequences?”
- “Does this serve my character’s arc?”

---

### 🏦 Forceful Bank Access

- Forcing someone to go into a bank and **withdraw all their money** is **not allowed**.
- However, in specific story-driven cases (e.g., **ransom**, **debt collection**, or **revenge**) you **may** ask someone to access their funds — but this must be:
  - Reasonable
  - Immersive
  - Not abusive or repetitive

> Staff will monitor this closely. If it becomes exploitative, the rule may be adjusted.

---

### 🧰 Breaking into Stashes

- You **may break into a stash** if the owner is **online**.
- There is a **cooldown** to prevent repeated break-ins.
- **Searching for stashes randomly to hit them without RP is fail RP.**
  - You must have **IC knowledge**, motivation, or reason to break into a stash.
  - Using stash raids to create story conflict is allowed — spammy or grindy behavior is not.

---

### 🔁 Final Rule of Thumb

**Each victim = one robbery**  
**Each robbery = one source**  
**Each robbery = a story, not a shortcut**

## powermeta

## Powergaming & Metagaming

Iron Range thrives on fair, believable roleplay. Powergaming and metagaming break immersion, rob other players of agency, and harm the collaborative spirit of the server. This section defines both, with clear expectations and examples.

---

### ⚙️ Powergaming

**Powergaming** is forcing an action on another player without giving them a chance to respond, or using game mechanics in a way that breaks realism or bypasses RP.

#### ❌ Examples of Powergaming:

- Forcing injuries, actions, or outcomes in /me or /do (e.g. `/me stabs you and kills you instantly`)
- Tying, cuffing, or knocking out a player without warning or RP lead-up
- Using mechanics (lasso, melee spam, execution animation) to win rather than roleplay
- Escaping restraints or recovering from injuries unrealistically
- Using the UI, mechanics, or emotes to avoid danger or cheat death (e.g. combat logging, instantly healing)

#### ✅ What To Do Instead:

- Use emotes that allow for response:  
  `/me attempts to push the man against the wall and reach for rope`
- Let others react before escalating
- Consider the story impact of your actions—not just the outcome

---

### 🧾 Metagaming

**Metagaming** is using information your character shouldn't know IC (in-character), but that you learned OOC (out-of-character).

#### ❌ Examples of Metagaming:

- Using player names or voice recognition when your character hasn’t met them
- Acting on information seen in Discord, streams, or another character’s perspective
- Tracking someone via map markers, Discord presence, or OOC clues
- Using stream chat or third-party communication to coordinate in-game actions

#### ✅ Keep It Immersive:

- If your character hasn’t heard, seen, or been told something IC, they don’t know it.
- Avoid using multiple characters to "gather" info that benefits another.
- Don’t mix character knowledge between alts.

---

### ⚖️ Enforcement & Intent

- Not all violations are intentional. Staff will always review context, impact, and history before acting.
- Repeated or obvious abuse of game mechanics, especially in conflict, will lead to warnings or bans.
- If you believe someone is metagaming or powergaming, **do not retaliate IC.**  
  Use a staff ticket to report it instead.

---

This is a roleplay server, not a competition.  
Play fair, write stories together, and leave room for others to shape the world with you.

## restraints

## Restraints, Cuffs & Escape

Mechanics like ropes, cuffs, and executions exist to enhance immersive roleplay—but they must be used **fairly**, **realistically**, and **with storytelling in mind**, not simply to dominate or "win."

### 🪢 Ropes & Lassos

- **Lassos should not be used as a first option** in PvP. They’re powerful tools and should reflect proper timing, not spam tactics.
- It is **NVL** to lasso an armed person who is actively shooting unless they are already subdued.
- Lassos are best used to:
  - Subdue someone after surrender
  - Detain someone fleeing unarmed
  - Enforce restraint after escalation

> **Note:** Flinging a rope from off-screen or while under fire just to down someone is considered **poor form** or powergaming.

---

### 🔗 Cuffs & Tying Players

- When cuffing or tying another player:

  - The person must be **surrendering or already restrained**.
  - There should be **clear RP** of the attempt—use `/me`, voice lines, or positioning.
  - Sudden ambush cuffing without any RP is considered **powergaming**.

- Law officers are expected to communicate cuffing actions (e.g., “I’m placing you in cuffs now”) and respond to attempts to resist.

---

### 💥 Execution & Grapple Animations

- Execution and grapple takedowns should be treated as **serious cinematic tools**—not “finisher moves” during combat.
- You should not:
  - Use them to speed-clear downed enemies
  - Abuse grapple to “cheese” a fight mid-gunplay
- If used during RP, ensure there is **intentional build-up**, not a surprise insta-takedown.

---

### 🏃 Escaping from Restraints

Escape is possible—but must follow rules that protect immersion and fairness.

You **may only break out of restraints** if:

1. **You are left alone or unsupervised**—your captors are no longer in view or earshot.
2. You **emote your intent** to escape, and the captors:
   - Explicitly allow it (e.g., “If you can get out of that, be my guest”)
   - Ignore the emote entirely, giving implicit consent
3. You’ve been moved to another location and your captors are distracted or split up.

> Escaping while in front of your captor or while on their horse is not allowed.

---

### 🗣️ Communication During Capture

- If you're tied or cuffed, you may still speak unless gagged in RP.
- If your captor roleplays gagging you, **emote muffled responses or nonverbal communication**.

---

These tools can create incredibly tense and emotional scenes when used with care.  
Give your captives scenes worth surviving—and your captors scenes worth remembering.

## Conflict Initiation & Shootout Policy

Conflict in Iron Range should always serve a **story-driven purpose**. Whether it's a tense standoff, a planned ambush, or a sudden betrayal—conflict must be **organic**, **intentional**, and **immersive**.

### 🎯 Initiating Conflict

- All PvP conflicts must begin with **clear roleplay**. This includes:

  - Dialogue, threats, or meaningful body language
  - Ultimatums or demands
  - A build-up that gives the other side context, even if minimal

- Conflict should never feel like it came **"out of nowhere"** with no story reason.
- **Intent matters**: Staff will always consider whether the RP felt earned or simply used as an excuse to fight.

#### ✅ Good Conflict Examples:

- “This is your last warning. Turn around or I’ll make sure you don’t walk out.”
- Drawing weapons after a heated exchange with buildup
- A tense negotiation that explodes into violence

#### ❌ Poor Conflict Examples:

- “Code word” attacks with no roleplay context
- Ambushing without any warning or RP
- Gunning someone down over petty slights with no escalation

---

### 💥 Shootout Expectations

Gunfights should feel **intense, dangerous, and meaningful**—not throwaway scenes.

#### During a shootout:

- **Gunfire should never be the first form of communication.**
- Give the opposing side time to react unless you’re escalating a long-standing conflict.
- Gunfights must be immersive: treat bullets, injuries, and death seriously.

#### Immersion tips:

- Avoid excessive yelling or metagaming mid-fight.
- Use period-appropriate language and callouts.
- Characters should behave like they value survival.

---

### 🚫 Abusing Combat Mechanics

Certain mechanics may give you an edge—but **exploiting them ruins RP**:

- **Fanning** should be used sparingly, and only when escalation has already occurred.
- **Execution animations** are NOT to be used during combat to “clean up” or gain unfair advantage.
- **Code word initiation** (e.g. saying “tulip” and shooting immediately) is only allowed if story tension is already high and the opposing side understands what's happening. Code words can be used to start a gunfight, not to immediately down opposing players.

---

### ⚖️ Fairness & Story

- **Conflict should feel fair**, even when the outcome isn't.
- If your group is significantly overpowering another, try to give them **a moment**, a **chance to surrender**, or a **scene worth remembering**.
- Don’t use PvP as a tool to win—use it to **elevate the narrative**.

---

Conflict drives the world forward—but only when done with purpose.  
Make it cinematic. Make it meaningful. Make it earned.

## Timeline Considerations

Iron Range takes place in an alternate universe where **the East is undiscovered** and **Horizon is a new frontier**, free of many of the real-world historical events. To support immersive and coherent storytelling, we ask that all players handle time progression with care.

### 🗓️ Timeline Fluidity

- Iron Range does **not follow a strict 1:1 time scale** with real life. Characters may experience time differently, and events can occur at a flexible pace to support story arcs.
- When discussing events in-character, use vague terms like:
  - “a while ago”
  - “just the other day”
  - “a long time back”
- Avoid saying exact durations (e.g., “10 years ago”) unless it makes narrative sense for everyone involved.

### ⛓️ Time in Jail or Injury

- If your character is jailed or incapacitated, you may **emote longer-term consequences** (e.g., "been locked up for months") but remember that others may not perceive the same time span.
- Don’t expect or demand others to adopt the same timeline as your character. Allow for differences in pacing.

### 👵 Character Aging

- Characters should age **organically over time**. Do not jump forward unrealistically.
- If a character has been benched for a long period (e.g., over 6 months in real time), you may age them slightly to reflect growth—but keep it reasonable.
- Children born during server events or through roleplay are **not allowed to be played** and cannot age into playable characters.

### 🚫 Avoid Timeline Conflict

- Don’t impose your character’s time logic on others. Example: If you say your character was in prison for 8 years, but others only remember the event from a few days ago, that's a disconnect.
- Always prioritize **consistency and immersion** over trying to enforce “real-time” tracking.

---

Iron Range is a story-first server. Time is a tool to build great narrative—not a system to be gamed or enforced. Keep it flexible, and always prioritize shared experience over personal canon.

## torture

## Consent in Executions & Torture RP

Iron Range is a collaborative storytelling server, and that includes darker themes—when done **maturely** and **respectfully**. Execution, torture, and mutilation roleplay must be treated with **serious intent** and require **clear OOC consent**.

### 🔪 Executions

- You **may not force another player to permanently kill their character (perma)**.
- Only the player being executed can decide whether the death is permanent or not.
- Executions should be used:
  - To end **long-term storylines**
  - During **high-stakes events** like betrayals, feuds, or justice
  - As a **climactic moment**, not casual clean-up
- **Execution animations** should only be used for meaningful scenes—not to “finish off” people during gunfights.
- Abusing the animation to down players or clear scenes will be considered **NVL or powergaming**.

> **Tip:** If you want a permanent death to occur, ask the other player OOC first. If they’re unsure, consider a coma or critical state instead.

---

### 🧷 Torture & Mutilation RP

Torture and mutilation roleplay can only happen if **all involved players give explicit OOC consent**.

#### ✅ What Requires Consent:

- Permanent injuries (e.g. eye loss, limb removal)
- Mutilation (branding, cutting off ears, carving symbols)
- Extended physical torment over hours/days
- Any RP that removes bodily autonomy

#### 🚫 What is Not Allowed:

- Springing surprise mutilation on a restrained player
- Ignoring a withdrawal of consent
- Using loopholes to force someone into accepting pain or injury

#### 🔒 How to Get Consent:

- Ask through Discord DM before beginning the scene, or by using the Discord Support Bot with staff.
- Discuss **boundaries**, **limits**, and **desired outcomes**.
- If consent is denied or withdrawn at any time, **you must stop immediately** or redirect the scene.

---

### 🧠 Staff Guidance & Tracking

- You may be asked to summarize or log extreme torture/mutilation scenes with staff if they have long-term consequences.
- If you’re not sure if something crosses a line, **ask staff first**.

---

This kind of RP can be powerful and memorable—but only when everyone is on board.  
Consent isn’t just a formality—it’s the foundation of meaningful storytelling.

## tribes

## 🪶 Tribal Roleplay & Cultural Groups in Iron Range

Tribes in Iron Range are rooted in **immersive storytelling, respectful cultural portrayal**, and the same core philosophy that applies to all groups in our world: **Everything must be earned through roleplay.**

Iron Range is set in the alternate-universe world of **Horizen**, where fictional Indigenous peoples thrive in unique ways, shaped by the land, their customs, and ongoing tensions with the changing world. While players may take inspiration from real Indigenous cultures, all tribes and practices should be original, grounded in care, and tailored to this fictional setting.

---

### 📖 Guidelines for Creating a Tribe

#### 1. 🧠 Foundation & Values

- Research Indigenous lifeways and beliefs to help inspire your fictional group, but do not replicate real-world sacred ceremonies or closed practices.
- Ask: What does your tribe value? What role does nature, spirit, and community play in their daily life?
- Keep your tribe’s beliefs internally consistent and immersive to Horizen’s setting.

#### 2. 🌄 Name & Territory

- Choose a name inspired by nature, animals, celestial elements, or spiritual concepts.
- Define your territory — do you reside near rivers, plains, caves, or sacred cliffs? These territories are not given. They must be roleplayed and **earned in-character**.

> **Note:** No land is officially assigned or granted by staff. All governance and control are determined via story and player interaction.

#### 3. 🔥 Traditions, Culture & Roles

- Develop **ceremonies, creation stories, and beliefs** that define your tribe’s worldview.
- Roles can include hunters, spiritual leaders, traders, warriors, and diplomats.
- Consider the tribe’s structure: Are you led by a council, a chieftain, a seer, or elders?

#### 4. 🧵 Craft & Identity

- Use in-game mechanics (like `item_labels`) to create symbolic items, clothing, or offerings.
- While we currently do not offer **tribal stashes or shared storages**, players may use personal stashes for inventory.
- Avoid costume-style outfits. Dress should reflect your character’s role and belief system.

#### 5. 🫱 External Relations

- Define how your tribe interacts with settlers, townsfolk, or other factions.
- Are you isolationist, adaptive, or diplomatic?
- Build RP around culture clash, cooperation, and tension — not domination or exclusion.

#### 6. ⚖️ Justice & Governance

- All leadership and internal justice systems must be handled through roleplay.
- Tribes may develop their own dispute resolution customs — exile, trials, ceremonies — but they hold no OOC authority or administrative power.

---

### 🪓 Storytelling & Group Presence

We **do not have fixed gang limits** or group caps. However:

- If a tribe becomes **unreasonably large** or **dominates** scenes unfairly, staff may request number balancing.
- Keep your presence strategic and story-focused. Powergaming through numbers is discouraged.

---

### 🏙️ Town & Hostile Scene Guidelines

While tribes may gather in large numbers in the world:

- **Use large gatherings to create meaningful narrative moments**, not to intimidate or escalate.
- If a scene turns hostile, **no more than 6 tribe members** may participate in the immediate conflict.
- Others must back off to prevent unfair stacking.

This applies to all scenes, not just tribes — these are general RP expectations.

---

### 🎯 Final Notes

Iron Range welcomes tribal roleplay that is immersive, collaborative, and respectful. Your tribe should feel **like it belongs** in Horizen: tied to the land, shaped by struggle, and open to the stories around it.

As always, if you're unsure about a portrayal or storyline, open a support ticket. We’re here to encourage powerful stories — not restrict creative expression.
