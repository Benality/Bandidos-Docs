## Native Character Roleplay in BandidosRP

In the world of BandidosRP, Indigenous cultures are alive, distinct, and integrated into the fictional world of Horizen. While we are an alternate universe with no direct ties to real-world history, we encourage rich and respectful portrayals of Native peoples—grounded in research, intention, and care.

### 🌎 Alternate Universe Disclaimer

BandidosRP is **not historical fiction**, and Horizen does not directly mirror real tribes or real events. However, inspiration from real Native cultures is welcome—as long as it’s done respectfully and without appropriation or stereotypes. All tribes in Horizen are fictional, but players are encouraged to create customs, beliefs, and roles that feel culturally consistent and immersive.

### 🚫 No Tolerance for Racism

We do **not allow racism**, hate speech, or derogatory portrayals—**in-character or out-of-character**. “Historical accuracy” is not a justification for bigotry. If you’re portraying conflict, it must be **consensual, story-driven, and respectful**.

Examples:

- ✅ You can explore cultural misunderstandings, tensions, or alliances with settlers if done maturely.
- ❌ You cannot use slurs, depict massacres, or treat Native characters as set dressing.

Offenses may result in immediate removal from the community.

### 🧬 Character Depth & Cultural Responsibility

Your Indigenous character should be a **full person**: flawed, evolving, and grounded in their beliefs or community. Avoid clichés like “stoic warrior” or “magical healer.”

Native characters can be:

- Hunters, traders, spiritual leaders, diplomats, artists, or outlaws.
- Members of tribes with original names and customs.
- Survivors who carry cultural teachings and adapt to the changing world.

### 🪶 Clothing, Names, and Culture

- Avoid costume-style outfits or randomly generated names like “Soaring Hawk.” Names and attire should reflect your character’s **tribal culture**, backstory, or life experience.
- War paint, headdresses, or ceremonial wear must have a **purpose**—spiritual, personal, or role-based. Don’t wear them casually in town.
- Long hair, braids, or beads may carry deep **spiritual or social meaning**. Consider what your character’s look says about their role or identity.

### 🛖 Tribes and Leadership

If you are forming or joining a tribe:

- Leadership is earned through respect and roleplay—not inherited or claimed by OOC status.
- Tribes should have internal structure, cultural traditions, and values.
- Land claims should come with storytelling and negotiation. Avoid declaring ownership without RP development.

### 🌽 Culture & Spirituality

- You may include food gathering, herbal medicine, ceremony, or oral storytelling as part of your roleplay.
- Healing roles (medicine people) must be earned, not self-appointed.
- Two-Spirit identities may be represented but should be handled with understanding and not conflated with Western terms for gender identity.

### 🗣 Language & Integration

- You may include bits of tribal language or invented language if it’s used meaningfully and sparingly.
- Outsiders joining a tribe should follow a slow, roleplay-driven process and **not take over tribal leadership or customs**.
- Mutual respect is key. Cultural gatekeeping or exclusion must be justified **in-character**, not used to avoid RP with others.

### ⚖️ Conflict RP

- Conflict should be approached like any other character arc: with nuance, purpose, and collaboration.
- Betrayals, rivalries, or land disputes must be **story-based**, not used to justify excessive aggression or isolation.

---

### 🔚 Final Notes

BandidosRP welcomes Native roleplay. We believe it can offer **beautiful, grounded, and powerful stories**. But with that opportunity comes responsibility. Don’t treat Native cultures as costume or aesthetic—**live them through immersive, thoughtful roleplay**.

If you're unsure about your portrayal, reach out to staff. We’re here to **support respectful creativity**, not to restrict it.
